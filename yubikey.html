01 — THE PROBLEM

SMS 2FA is broken at the protocol level.

SIM-swap attacks: an attacker convinces your carrier to port your number to their SIM, then receives every SMS code. Coinbase has been the source of multiple eight-figure SIM-swap losses since 2022. SMS was never a security layer — it was a convenience layer pretending to be one.

02 — THE FIX

Hardware-bound, phish-proof.

YubiKey speaks FIDO2/WebAuthn — the modern 2FA standard. To approve a login, you tap a physical button on the key. The cryptographic challenge is bound to the actual website, so phishing pages can't replay the response. SMS can be intercepted; this can't.

Common questions

Things people ask before they buy.

Which model should I get?

The YubiKey 5C NFC ($55) is the right answer for most people — USB-C plus NFC for phone tap. If you have older USB-A laptops, the YubiKey 5 NFC works the same way. The Security Key Series ($25) is cheaper but lacks features like OTP and OpenPGP. Don't overthink it — the 5C NFC covers every use case for a crypto holder.

How many do I need?

Two. Always two. One stays on your keychain or in your pocket, the other lives in your fire safe as the recovery key. Enroll both on every account at the same time. Lose the primary? The backup unlocks everything. Lose both? You still have the per-account recovery codes you wrote down. Single-key setups are how people get locked out forever.

What if I lose them both?

That's why every site that supports 2FA also gives you backup recovery codes when you enroll. Print those, fold them, put them in the safe with the YubiKey. The hierarchy: YubiKey first, backup YubiKey second, recovery codes third. Three-layer redundancy for the cost of $110 plus a piece of paper.

Does my exchange support it?

Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, Gemini, Coinbase Pro, Bitstamp, OKX, Bybit — yes. Most major US and EU exchanges. Some smaller / Asian-only exchanges still only support SMS or TOTP — a YubiKey can do TOTP too via the Yubico Authenticator app, so you're covered there as well.

Is this NFC or USB?

The 5 NFC and 5C NFC do both — plug into a laptop via USB or tap to a phone via NFC. Same key, two ways to authenticate. That's why those models are worth the extra $5-10 over the cheaper variants.
