01 — MONETARY HISTORY

The Bitcoin Standard

Saifedean Ammous · 2018

The "why" of Bitcoin from a serious economist. Walks through the history of money — gold, fiat, the gold standard, central banking — and lands on hard money as the missing constraint. After this book, "store of value" stops being marketing and starts being a coherent argument. Required reading.

02 — TECHNICAL FOUNDATION

Mastering Bitcoin

Andreas Antonopoulos · 2nd ed 2017

The "how" of Bitcoin. Covers keys, addresses, scripts, mining, consensus — the actual mechanics. Technical but accessible; you don't need to be a developer to read it, but you'll come out understanding why self-custody matters at the protocol level. Written 30 years ago. Predicted Bitcoin without naming it, predicted remote work, predicted nation-state revenue collapse from digital wealth. Common questions

What people ask about the reading list.

Which one should I read first?

The Bitcoin Standard. It's the most accessible, gives you the framework, and primes you for the others. Then Layered Money for the structural view, then The Sovereign Individual for the power view, and Mastering Bitcoin last when you're ready to go technical.

Are these too technical?

Three of the four are non-technical and read like normal books. Mastering Bitcoin is the technical one — it explains code and cryptography. You can absolutely skip the deepest chapters and still get 80% of the value. None require math beyond high school.

Is this a course in disguise?

No. There's no upsell, no Discord, no $997 follow-up program. These are real books from real publishers. Author royalties on Amazon are a few dollars each — the people who wrote them weren't trying to sell you anything else.

Audiobook or paperback?

The Bitcoin Standard and The Sovereign Individual work great as audiobooks for commutes. Mastering Bitcoin and Layered Money have diagrams, footnotes, and structure that benefit from a physical copy. Buy in whichever format you'll actually finish.

I've read these before. What's the refresher pick?

Reread Layered Money in 2026. The framework about stablecoins as layer-2 dollars is sharper now than when it was written. The other three age well too, but Layered Money is the one that gets *more* relevant every year as tokenized treasuries and digital dollars roll out.
