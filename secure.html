Common questions

Things traders actually ask before buying.

Which Ledger should I get?

For most traders the Nano S Plus at $79 is enough — supports all major chains, plugs in via USB-C, holds keys for thousands of tokens. Upgrade to Nano X (~$149) only if you want Bluetooth + more app slots, or to Stax / Flex if you want a touchscreen. Don't overspend on features you won't use.

What if Ledger goes out of business?

Doesn't matter. Your 24-word recovery phrase is the wallet, not the device. You can restore that phrase on any other BIP-39 hardware wallet (Trezor, Coldcard, BitBox) or even on a paper wallet. Ledger is the convenient screen, not the asset.

Is it actually safer than a software wallet?

By a wide margin for any non-trivial balance. Software wallets sit on a phone or computer that can be phished, malware'd, or compromised by a single bad browser extension. The hardware wallet's signing keys never leave the secure element — even a fully-owned host can't steal them.

How much should I have on it before it's worth $79?

Honest answer: as soon as your crypto is worth more than the wallet, the math is in your favor. If you're holding $500+ and it's not in cold storage, you're paying a much higher implicit insurance premium against hot-wallet risk than $79.

Do I lose access to staking, DeFi, etc.?

No. Ledger Live and any wallet that supports hardware signers (MetaMask, Phantom, Rabby) work fine — you just confirm each transaction physically. Staking via Ledger Live works for ETH, SOL, ATOM, DOT, and others natively.
