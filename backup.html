Common questions

What traders ask before buying.

Which steel plate should I get?

The two most-used in the space are the Cryptosteel Capsule ($80-100, tile-based — slot in pre-made letter tiles) and the Billfodl ($50-70, similar tile design). For pure DIY, a blank steel plate with a hammer-stamp kit runs about $40-60 on Amazon — same protection, no fancy enclosure. Pick based on whether you want plug-and-play or a manual project.

Do I really need this if I have a Ledger?

Yes. The Ledger is the convenient signing device — it can break, get lost, or be confiscated. The 24-word recovery phrase is what reconstitutes the wallet on any new BIP-39 device. If the phrase is on paper and the paper burns, your coins are gone permanently. Steel removes that single point of failure.

What about Shamir Backup or splitting the phrase?

Shamir (SLIP-39) splits the secret into N shards where any K can reconstitute it. Useful for high-value setups across multiple locations. For most traders, a single steel plate stored in a fire safe or safe-deposit box is fine. Add Shamir only if you genuinely need geographic redundancy and trust the math.

Where do I store it?

Three reasonable options: home fire safe (cheap, fast), bank safe-deposit box (secure but US banks are not always crypto-friendly with seizure history), or a trusted-relative-in-a-different-state setup. Don't overthink it. Most traders use a fire safe under $200.

Can someone find my plate and steal my coins?

Yes — physical security matters. Keep it in a locked safe, don't tell anyone what it looks like or where it is, and consider a passphrase ("25th word") layer in your wallet for extra protection. The plate alone is the equivalent of cash — treat it that way.
